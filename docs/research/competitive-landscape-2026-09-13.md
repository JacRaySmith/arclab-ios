# Competitive landscape and "obvious feedback" patterns — 2026-09-13

Scope: products that measure basketball shooting (or adjacent single-phone motion analysis), what
players and coaches say they want, presentation patterns that make a measurement land, evidence that
any of it improves shooting, and privacy notes for minors. Every factual claim carries a URL.
Anything not verifiable from a fetched page is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Reddit could not be fetched
from this environment (both `old.reddit.com` and `www.reddit.com` are blocked for the fetch tool, and
web search returned no Reddit threads); user quotes below come from App Store review pages, vendor
pages, trade press and published papers instead.

## Summary — where the gap is

The market splits cleanly in two. **Fixed hardware** (Noah, RSPCT, ShotTracker, the shooting
machines) owns absolute accuracy and real-time audio, at $3,300–$45,000 plus service fees, and is
sold to programs, not players. **Phone apps** (HomeCourt, Ball AI, Noah's own HOOPS, Ballogy, and a
2025–26 wave of "AI coach" apps) are cheap, but they compete on *counting* — makes, misses,
percentages, heat maps, gamified drills — and their single most common complaint is that the count is
wrong (false attempts from dribbling, missed makes, orange-ball-only, overheating). None of the phone
apps we could inspect reports shot-to-shot *variance* with a stated n, separates a player's own makes
from misses on any measured metric, tests for within-session drift, or shows the fit over the
player's own video with an honest confidence. The only product that treats consistency as the
product is Noah ("shows you how consistent your shot is" — Tony Bennett testimonial,
https://www.noahbasketball.com/results), and its consumer-priced path is either a $3,349+ backboard
sensor or a phone app limited to straight-ahead shots. The gap ArcLab targets is real: a
per-(shot-type, distance) consistency number, evidence-backed findings, and a traced arc on the
player's own footage — none of which requires the absolute accuracy the incumbents sell.

A second gap is trust. HomeCourt, Ball AI and HOOPS all fail the same way: users cannot tell *why* a
shot was miscounted, so a single bad session poisons confidence in every number. Showing the work
(tracked ball, fitted arc, rejected frames, tier label) is a differentiator no incumbent app offers.

## Presentation patterns that make feedback obvious

1. **One number per rep, spoken or shown big (Noah audio callout).** Noah "literally tells you what the entry angle was" after each shot; users build a feel for the number. https://fansided.com/2018/04/24/nylon-calculus-noah-fix-every-jumpshot/ — *ArcLab:* in the make/miss review strip, show one large number per clip (entry angle, colour-banded); optionally speak it during replay. Never more than one number in the strip.
2. **Target as a band, not a point (Noah 45 ±2°, "Splashzone ±3"").** https://www.noahbasketball.com/methodology — *ArcLab:* the band is the player's own (median ± MAD within the cell), drawn as a shaded region; the universal 43–47° appears only as a faint reference labelled "published".
3. **Consistency = standard deviation, named and compared across conditions (TrackMan).** TrackMan's Consistency number is the SD per parameter; the Justin Rose example compares SD at 60/70/80 yards to pick what to practise. https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/how-to-utilize-the-consistency-number — *ArcLab:* show entry/release-angle SD per (type, distance) cell side by side, and rank cells by SD so the player sees *where* they are loose.
4. **Translate spread into outcome units (Noah: 2° at 55° → 15.5" depth spread; at 45° → <4").** https://www.noahbasketball.com/blog/the-science-of-shooting-arc — *ArcLab:* convert the player's own angle SD into inches of depth spread at the rim using the fitted trajectory; "your ±3.8° is ±9 inches at the rim" is obvious where "SD 3.8°" is not.
5. **Spatial dispersion plot coloured by outcome (Noah Rim Map, TrackMan jellybean, Rapsodo overhead dispersion).** Green/light-green/red dots at rim entry. https://www.noahbasketball.com/blog/the-rim-map-noahlytics-data-service · https://golfstead.com/rapsodo-mobile-launch-monitor-review — *ArcLab:* a 1-D depth strip (side view) or 2-D rim map (frontal view) of entry points, makes vs misses, with n and tier printed on it.
6. **Three-tier disclosure; "each tile is a doorway, not a destination" (WHOOP).** Overview → trend → raw graphs. https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown — *ArcLab:* session card (3 findings) → finding page (evidence numbers, plot) → the clips that produced them.
7. **One semantic colour vocabulary (WHOOP green/yellow/red).** Same source. — *ArcLab:* one triple used everywhere: inside your band / drifting / outside; never reuse those colours for anything else (tier labels get a different treatment).
8. **Personal baseline with a short window against a long one (Oura: 14-day deviation vs 2-month trend; WHOOP needs ~30 days before baselines).** https://livity-app.com/en/blog/readiness-score-explained · https://longevity-protocols.com/en/knowledge-base/diagnostics/whoop-band/ — *ArcLab:* "this session vs your last 30 days" in the same cell, and an honest "collecting baseline: 18/30" progress bar rather than a spinner.
9. **Personal records, framed as competing with yourself (Strava Best Efforts: top-3 PRs per benchmark).** https://stories.strava.com/articles/track-your-run-prs — *ArcLab:* "tightest 20-shot window" and "best session SD" records per cell; no leaderboards (they invite the cheating HomeCourt users complain about).
10. **Goal-hit feedback per rep against a user-set target (Sportsbox "instantly tells you whether you hit your goal").** https://golficity.com/sportsbox-ai-review-turn-your-phone-into-a-3d-golf-coach/ — *ArcLab:* let the player set a target band for one metric (e.g. entry angle 44–48°) and show hit/miss ticks along the session timeline; the target defaults to their own make-band.
11. **Before/after arc overlay and side-by-side (Noah arc plots; OnForm overlay compare).** https://www.noahbasketball.com/before_after · https://onform.com/ — *ArcLab:* overlay the mean traced arc from session A on session B with shaded spread, and auto-pick the player's best-vs-worst shot pair for a side-by-side.
12. **Let the learner pull feedback rather than push it (motor-learning evidence).** Self-controlled video feedback produced better form on transfer (p = .04, η² = .15) even though learners asked for it on only 27% of trials, falling from 33% to 19%. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3438820/ — *ArcLab:* no auto-playing tips; findings are one tap away, and the app remembers which finding the player chose to open.
13. **Anti-pattern to avoid: an unexplained score.** SIQ's 61° "release angle" and the 2025–26 apps' "shooting score" with no method shown. https://www.inverse.com/input/reviews/siq-smart-basketball-bluetooth-app-nba-ball-review — *ArcLab:* every number links to the frames and the fit residual that produced it; a number that cannot be shown is `nil` with a reason (CLAUDE.md rule 1).

## Evidence that these products improve shooting

- **Noah (vendor).** "Identical team members shot 17% better during the six weeks of the season they trained with a Noah Shooting Aid" — no n, design or control published. https://www.noahbasketball.com/before_after. The 2010 magazine reprint gives modelled FT percentages by arc (avg shooter 68% at 45° vs 57% at 53° vs 56% at 35°; "very skilled" 96/89/80) and two coach anecdotes (team FT 58% → 74%) without controls. https://www.noahbasketball.com/hs-fs/file-649020446-pdf/PDFs/building-the-perfect-arc.pdf?t=1396974603000. The "results" page is testimonials only ("No control groups, baseline data, or scientific methodology"). https://www.noahbasketball.com/results. The often-repeated "40,000-shot Auburn/Gonzaga study" could not be located on any Noah page fetched; the only Gonzaga link is Jerry Krause's affiliation in the 2010 article — **UNVERIFIED**. Design quality: promotional, uncontrolled; the geometric spread-vs-angle argument is sound physics, the outcome claims are not evidence.
- **RSPCT, ShotTracker, Dr. Dish, Shoot-A-Way, HomeCourt, Ball AI:** no published or vendor improvement study found.
- **Independent, feedback-in-general:**
  - Real-time arc visualization (2D display vs AR headset), 9 experienced shooters: "shot angle consistency improved throughout the study in both, the 2D and AR conditions"; participants "actively used it to refine their subsequent shots". Small n, no retention test. https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04118
  - Self-controlled video feedback on the basketball set shot, 28 novices: better form on transfer (η² = .153); accuracy equal between groups. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3438820/
  - AR real-time feedback in youth soccer (n = 60, 12–15 y): shooting d = 1.40, motivation d ≈ 0.9–1.1; authors flag a possible novelty effect and lack of follow-up. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1661936/full
  - NBA 2023–24 tracking data: terminal ball-path curvature is negatively associated with FT performance and "explains much more of the between-player variance" than early-motion curvature — a between-player correlation, not an intervention. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13779
  - CHI 2025 "knowledge-based SOP" AI feedback for beginners' shooting mechanics: exists, full text blocked (403) — **UNVERIFIED** findings. https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713324
- **Takeaway:** the only defensible claims are (a) immediate, quantitative feedback on arc improves arc *consistency* in small studies, and (b) learners do fine when they control feedback frequency. Nobody has shown a phone app improves game shooting percentage. ArcLab should not claim it either; it should claim measured consistency change, with n.

## Privacy and regulatory notes (minors, video, pose)

- **COPPA (US, under 13).** FTC's final amendments were published 2025-04-22, effective 2025-06-23, with compliance required by 2026-04-22; "personal information" now includes biometric identifiers such as "gait patterns, facial templates, or faceprints". https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule · https://securiti.ai/ftc-coppa-final-rule-amendments/. The FTC FAQ states an operator is not collecting personal information "simply because your app interacts with personal information that is stored on the device and is never transmitted". https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions. ArcLab's zero-network design (CLAUDE.md rule 6) is therefore the compliance strategy; any future export/share must be user-initiated and must not route through an ArcLab server without revisiting this.
- **Illinois BIPA.** Biometric identifiers are retina/iris scans, fingerprints, voiceprints, and "scans of hand or facial geometry"; private right of action with $1,000–$5,000 statutory damages per violation; the 2024 amendment (SB 2979, signed 2024-08-02) limits recovery to one violation per person per collection method and accepts electronic signatures for consent. https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/8/bipa-update-illinois-limits-liability-and-clarifies-electronic-consent-for-biometric-data-collection · https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/data-privacy-laws/bipa/. Body-pose keypoints are not listed identifiers, but do not run face-geometry or voice models, do not store face templates, and keep a written retention/deletion policy (delete-all in settings) to stay clearly outside scope.
- **Apple App Review.** 1.3 Kids Category: no third-party analytics/advertising, no PII or device info to third parties; 5.1.4: apps "intended primarily for kids should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising"; 5.1.1 requires purpose strings that "clearly and completely describe your use of the data" for camera/photos. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/. Recommendation: do not opt into the Kids Category (it is for young children and restricts links/purchases); rate 12+ or 4+ as appropriate, ship no analytics SDK, and word the camera/photo-library purpose strings around on-device analysis.

## Ranked product ideas

Ranked by (value to a shooter trying to improve) × (feasibility on one phone, on-device). 1 = do first.

1. **Traced arc over the player's own clip at n = 1, with release and entry angle labelled and the fit residual visible.** Players say arc is what they watch (Harvard interviews, https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04118); no phone app shows the fit. Satisfies the cold-start gate and is the trust anchor for every later number.
2. **Consistency number per (type, distance) cell, translated into inches at the rim.** TrackMan's SD-as-consistency (https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/how-to-utilize-the-consistency-number) plus Noah's spread argument (https://www.noahbasketball.com/blog/the-science-of-shooting-arc). Cheap to compute, the metric users quote back (SIQ review), and nobody ships it per cell.
3. **Make/miss split plots from manual tagging: entry angle and depth, makes vs misses, with n and Cohen's d.** Tre Jones' "front rim or back rim" (https://matthewtynan.substack.com/p/noahs-arc-in-search-of-jeremy-sochans) is the question players ask; the swipe-to-tag strip gives clean ground truth for v2 auto-detection.
4. **Within-session drift timeline (metric vs rep index, with a trend test and a "you loosen after rep ~60" flag).** Nearly free once shots are indexed; no competitor does it; matches the 200–500-rep user.
5. **Setup-quality preflight: a 10-second check that predicts the tier (A/B/C) before the session starts.** Directly attacks the top complaint category (miscounts, overlay squares, ball colour, tripod height) across HomeCourt, Ball AI and HOOPS (see "What users say" 5–9). Rim-detection confidence, frame rate and stability are already computed for tiering.
6. **Evidence-first findings card (max 3), each tapping through to the clips and the numbers.** WHOOP's tiered "doorway" architecture (https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown) applied to the §6 rules engine; the copy is "associated with", never "why".
7. **Session-vs-baseline bands with an honest "collecting baseline 18/30" progress indicator.** Oura/WHOOP baseline framing (https://livity-app.com/en/blog/readiness-score-explained); avoids the fabricated-precision problem the 2025–26 "shooting score" apps have.
8. **Personal records per cell ("tightest 20-shot window", "lowest session SD"), no leaderboards.** Strava Best Efforts (https://stories.strava.com/articles/track-your-run-prs); leaderboards attract the cheating HomeCourt users complain about.
9. **User-set target band with per-rep hit ticks during review (defaults to the player's own make-band).** Sportsbox's "did you hit your goal" loop (https://golficity.com/sportsbox-ai-review-turn-your-phone-into-a-3d-golf-coach/); post-capture only, so no real-time constraint.
10. **Shareable evidence card (image) for a coach, generated on-device, no account.** Ballogy's recruiting-resume angle (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ballogy-basketball-training/id1276974145) without the upload; keeps rule 6 intact and gives the parent/coach something concrete.

Deferred/rejected: real-time audio callouts (Noah's signature; excluded by the v1 non-goal and by the HomeCourt "difficult to hear from over 5 metres" problem); universal form grades (contested methodology — Pro Shot critics, Okogie on floaters); any metric that cannot show its frames (rule 1).
